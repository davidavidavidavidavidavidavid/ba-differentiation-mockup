## Unit 1: Factors and Multiples

**Question 1** (3 pts) — CCSS: 4.OA.B.4, 6.NS.B.4
The product of four different natural numbers is 105. What is the sum of the four numbers?
*Answer: 16 (1×3×5×7)*

**Question 2** (3 pts) — CCSS: 6.RP.A.1, 4.OA.A.2
In a park there are some cats and dogs. The number of cats' feet equals the number of dogs' noses. The number of cats is ___ of the number of dogs.
*Answer: a quarter the size*

**Question 3** (5 pts) — CCSS: 6.NS.B.4, MP.1
All factors of a number N (excluding 1 and N) are written down. The biggest factor is 55 times the smallest. For how many numbers N is this true?
*Answer: 3 (N = 220, 495, or 1375)*

**Question 4** (5 pts) — CCSS: 6.NS.B.4, MP.1
Writing different positive whole numbers less than 13 in a row so adjacent numbers always divide each other, what's the max amount that could be written?
*Answer: 11*

**Question 5** (4 pts) — CCSS: 6.NS.B.4, 5.NF.A.1
Grandma doesn't know if 4, 6, or 9 grandchildren will come today. Into how many pieces must she cut the cake to guarantee an equal share for whoever shows up?
*Answer: 36*

**Question 6** (4 pts) — CCSS: 6.RP.A.3, 4.OA.A.3
Sewing 4 short strips into one long strip takes 15 minutes. How long for 7 short strips?
*Answer: 30 minutes*

**Question 7** (3 pts) — CCSS: 4.OA.C.5, MP.7
Painting the word BEASTLY, one letter a day starting Monday, on which day does he paint the last letter?
*Answer: Sunday*

**Question 8** (5 pts) — CCSS: 6.NS.B.4, MP.1
Six years ago Priya's age was a multiple of 9; in nine years it'll be a multiple of 6. Nine years ago Sam's age was a multiple of 6; in six years it'll be a multiple of 9. What's the smallest possible positive age difference between them?
*Answer: 6*

**Question 9** (4 pts) — CCSS: MP.1, 4.OA.B.4
Four cards show 5, 8, 16, 36. Four back-labels read 'multiple of 9', 'multiple of 4', 'greater than 10', 'negative number' — each number's label must NOT describe it, every number/label used once. Which number gets 'negative number'?

**Question 10** (4 pts) — CCSS: 4.OA.B.4, 6.NS.B.4

**Question 12** (4 pts) — CCSS: 4.OA.B.4, 6.NS.B.4

**Question 13** (5 pts) — CCSS: 4.OA.B.4, 6.NS.B.4

**Question 14** (5 pts) — CCSS: 4.MD.A.1, 4.MD.A.2

**Question 15** (5 pts) — CCSS: MP.1, 4.OA.A.3

**Question 16** (3 pts) — CCSS: 4.MD.A.2, MP.1

**Question 17** (5 pts) — CCSS: MP.1, 4.OA.C.5

**Question 18** (5 pts) — CCSS: 4.OA.C.5, 8.F.B.4
A sequence starts −1, 1, −1, 1; each new term is the product of the two before it. What's the sum of the first 1,500 terms?

**Question 19** (4 pts) — CCSS: MP.1, 4.OA.A.3

**Question 20** (4 pts) — CCSS: 4.NBT.A.2, 4.OA.B.4

**Question 21** (4 pts) — CCSS: 4.MD.A.2, 6.EE.B.7

**Question 22** (5 pts) — CCSS: 4.MD.A.2, 6.RP.A.3

**Question 23** (5 pts) — CCSS: MP.1, 4.OA.B.4

**Question 1** (5 pts) — CCSS: MP.1, HSF.BF.A.2

## Unit 5: Division

**Question 1** (4 pts)
Marisol picks a number, divides it by 4, adds 9, then multiplies the result by 6. She gets 90. What number did she start with?
*Answer: 24*

**Question 2** (5 pts)
A four-digit number uses the digits 1, 2, 3, and 6, each exactly once. The number is divisible by 4. How many such numbers are there?
*Answer: 8*

**Question 3** (5 pts)
The five-digit number 36X2Y is divisible by 4, 5, and 9. What is X + Y?
*Answer: 7 (X=7, Y=0)*

**Question 4** (4 pts)
In Mr. Diaz's class, there are twice as many girls as boys. There are 24 children in the class. How many boys are there?
*Answer: 8*

**Question 5** (5 pts)
A basketball team earns 2 points for a win, 1 point for a tie, and 0 points for a loss. The team played 30 games and earned 43 points. What is the greatest number of games the team could have lost?
*Answer: 8*

**Question 6** (3 pts)
A birthday cake weighs 960 g. Marcus cuts it into 4 pieces. The biggest piece weighs exactly as much as the other three combined. How much does the biggest piece weigh?
*Answer: 480 g*

**Question 7** (4 pts)
18 students signed up for chess club. Then 15 more joined. The coach wants 5 equal-sized teams. How many more students need to join?
*Answer: 2*

**Question 8** (5 pts)
N is the smallest positive whole number such that half of N is divisible by 2, one-third by 3, one-fourth by 4, one-fifth by 5, one-sixth by 6, and one-eighth by 8. How many digits does the square root of N have?
*Answer: 3 digits (N = 14,400, √N = 120)*
